Summer is on. May closed with the fastest Spokane sales pace in nearly a year (14-day median cumulative DOM), Kootenai's median pushed past $600K for the first time since last summer, and June brings Hoopfest, Car d'Lane, Ironman 70.3, and a stack of patio-season openings. Here is what the May data told us and what to watch as the summer window unfolds.
Market Snapshot
Where the Market Stands Right Now

May is the latest fully reported month, and the data shifted decisively away from the buyer-friendly Q1 pattern. Spokane closed 546 homes at a 14-day median cumulative DOM, Kootenai cleared 271 closings, and the 30-year mortgage rate finished the month at 6.53% per Freddie Mac PMMS. The Kootenai median pushed past $600K for the first time since June 2025; Spokane's median ticked back to $450K after a slow first quarter.

For Sellers
5 Things Every Seller Should Know in June
1 Price-on-arrival wins. Spokane sellers who landed at or just under comp gave up only 0.08% from list and closed in 14 days. Those who reduced mid-listing absorbed an average 1.16% cut. That 1% gap is real money on a $450K home.
2 List into the summer window, not after it. Closed-sale volume is highest June–August in both counties. Listing early-to-mid June puts you in front of the largest buyer pool of the year before school-start pressure forces decisions.
3 The first weekend matters more than the next four. With months-supply at 2.4 (Spokane) and 2.7 (Kootenai), the under-$600K market still leans to sellers. Stack showings into the first 72 hours.
4 Price for the band, not the headline. The $400K–$499K band was Spokane's largest in May (150 closings, 28%). Sitting on the wrong side of a round number ($510K vs $499K) can cost you the bulk of the buyer pool.
5 Concessions are negotiable; price cuts are remembered. A $5,000 rate buydown or closing-cost credit moves a deal without resetting your listing history. Lead with concessions; hold the line on price until the data forces a change.
For First-Time Buyers
Your Window Just Got Narrower
1 Pre-approved with a local lender, not just a quote. Listing agents take offers with local lender contacts first. Out-of-state shops add 24–48 hours of friction when a 14-day DOM means the home is closing before they call back.
2 FHA is still 15% of Spokane closings. 83 of 546 May closings were FHA, and it still wins under $400K. If you're using FHA, target homes that have been listed 21+ days where sellers are open to non-cash offers.
3 Post Falls and Hayden are the affordability story. Kootenai's median crossed $602K, but most under-$500K inventory is in Post Falls and Hayden — newer construction, quick I-90 access. Tour those first.
4 Rate buydowns are back on the table. With the 30-year near 6.5%, sellers are open to 2-1 buydowns as a concession. A 2-1 drops your Year-1 rate to ~4.5%, freeing ~$300–$400/mo on a $400K loan. Ask.
5 Inspect for what insurance will charge you for. Carriers are pricing wildfire risk, roof age, and panel type (Federal Pacific, Zinsco). Pull an insurance quote on the actual property before you fall in love — an extra $1,500/yr is a hidden price increase.
Watch Out For
What's Worth Watching Into Summer
⚠ Yun cut his 2026 sales forecast from 14% to 4%. NAR's chief economist downgraded existing-home-sales growth by 10 points, citing rates and the oil-price shock. May volume fell 21% YoY in Spokane, 13% in Kootenai.
⚠ Wildfire-season insurance reviews start now. May–October is the carrier review window. Some are non-renewing in wildland-urban-interface zones (Five Mile, Mead, north Kootenai). Request a renewal quote in June, not August.
⚠ Kootenai inventory is concentrated above $600K. Only 67 of 271 May closings (25%) were under $500K. The median crossed $600K because the affordable inventory cleared first — watch whether builders backfill the under-$500K layer.
⚠ Mortgage rates trended up through May. The 30-year moved from 6.36% (May 14) to 6.53% (May 28) per Freddie Mac — ~$35/mo more on a $400K loan. Run June payments at 6.75% to leave room.
